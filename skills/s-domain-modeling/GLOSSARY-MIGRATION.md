# Adopt glossary names without losing project knowledge

`GLOSSARY.md` and `GLOSSARY-MAP.md` are the defaults for new projects. An existing project glossary convention remains authoritative; a skill update alone does not authorize a repository-wide migration.

When legacy `CONTEXT.md` or `CONTEXT-MAP.md` exists, identify its content and current readers before writing a new glossary. Continue using the established glossary until the project adopts another path. Read the existing map to locate context-specific glossaries; neither a filename nor a root file proves that every context shares one vocabulary.

For an adopted migration, preserve definitions, avoided synonyms, context boundaries, relationships and provenance. A glossary-only legacy file can move to the corresponding new name; a mixed file needs its vocabulary separated while its specifications, decisions and operational knowledge remain in their established homes or move to identified durable records. Verify that destination coverage preserves the source before retiring any content. Keep accepted direction distinct from observed implementation.

If old and new files coexist, resolve their authority from project instructions, accepted decisions and content. Merge compatible vocabulary into one canonical glossary per context. Keep unresolved meanings outside accepted definitions and request the person's choice when the remaining difference is consequential. Do not choose a winner by filename or silently overwrite either source.

Update the map, steering instructions and known links together. Check that readers reach the intended glossary and that context-specific terms stay in their own contexts. Keep an old-path pointer while a known reader still requires it; it points to the canonical file instead of retaining a second editable copy. Preserve historical runs and frozen evidence under their original names.

Migration evidence identifies the chosen convention, source-to-destination coverage, affected readers, checked links and any unresolved content or ownership. A missing history or unresolved reader keeps that part of the migration open; it does not justify discarding knowledge or blocking unrelated work.
