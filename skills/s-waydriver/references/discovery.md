# Autonomous Discovery

Use discovery to sharpen the destination, surface conflicts across the whole domain, and expose the first safe frontier without manufacturing questions.

Start from repository and project evidence. For each consequential unknown, state what decision it changes and assess whether evidence, current practice, specialist knowledge, or a bounded experiment can resolve it. Use `smart-shot` for consequential intent or quality ambiguity before treating the destination as specified, as well as substantial legwork; otherwise use available primary sources and bounded experiments. When discovery changes domain concepts, language, relationships or rules, apply [domain modeling](domain-modeling.md).

A resolved decision states what its evidence supports and what remains assumed. Evidence from analogous cases does not establish the destination-specific outcome; unsupported conclusions remain hypotheses. Reversible conclusions supported by current evidence may be accepted autonomously; consequential gaps earn more evidence, an experiment, or an explicit unresolved boundary. Ask the person one focused question only when the remaining uncertainty is consequential and the answer is genuinely their preference, authority, or unavailable knowledge.

For unresolved knowledge, record the decision it controls and the next observation that could change it. Resolve independent questions directly; route interacting competence gaps through `smart-shot`. Track progress by decisions supported or rejected, not by the number of sources, topics or uncertainty labels.

Breadth-first charting looks across the destination before deepening one branch. Unconditional grilling is waste: stop discovering when the route is clear enough to state acceptance-bearing work. Preserve unresolved but in-scope uncertainty as fog.

Resolve uncertainty with proportionate legwork before asking. Existing intent, design, relevant practice and reversible demonstrations can support a provisional choice, including when the person cannot articulate a preference in advance. Ask when plausible answers would materially change the next commitment and investigation cannot reasonably settle it; explain the consequence in terms the person can judge. Otherwise state the assumption or evidence gap and proceed within the authorized scope. Missing feedback alone does not require an interview. Permission to take an action is a separate boundary from confidence in its value.

Discover delivery capabilities when the available means could materially change the intended outcome. Verify relevant alternatives and distinguish a real constraint from missing access, unfamiliarity or an untested assumption. Before accepting a material reduction of the outcome to fit current tools, present the verified trade-offs and a recommendation for the person to choose; retain existing authorization and continue work that does not preempt that choice. Routine equivalent implementation choices remain delegated.
